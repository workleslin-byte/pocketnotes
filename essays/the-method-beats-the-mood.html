The Method Beats the Mood

Two lines, every day. The smallest reliable habit beats the loudest one. On showing up before you feel like it.

Tuesday morning, six-fifty, the light still grey through the window and nothing coming. The notebook is open. The pen is in hand. There is no idea, no energy, no particular reason to write anything. There is only the fact of the open notebook and the habit of opening it.

Two lines get written. They are not good lines. They are not the lines of someone inspired. They are the lines of someone honouring an agreement with themselves that predates this particular morning by several months — an agreement that says: two lines, regardless. Not good lines. Not finished lines. Just two lines.

This is the method. It is not glamorous. It has nothing to recommend it except that it works.

✦   ✦   ✦

The mood is unreliable. Every writer has known this and most have tried to manage it — to arrange conditions, to wait for the right state, to protect the morning or the evening or the particular quality of light that seems to make things possible. The arrangements are understandable. They are also, eventually, the problem. The more conditions you attach to showing up, the more conditions can fail to be met. One bad night of sleep, one difficult morning, one week where the conditions are wrong, and the habit dissolves. What’s left is not a writer. It’s a person who used to write when things were going well.

The method asks for something different: show up anyway. Do the minimum. Two lines. Not because two lines will change anything, but because the person who writes two lines on a bad Tuesday is the person who has a notebook full of pages by December. The accumulation is arithmetic, not inspiration.

“Amateurs sit and wait for inspiration. The rest of us just get up and go to work.”

— Stephen King, On Writing, 2000

There is a useful distinction between the habit and the output. The output on any given day may be nothing. A date. A fragment. A question that doesn’t go anywhere. The habit, maintained, is everything — because it keeps the channel open. The days when something actually arrives have to find you already there, already in place, pen in hand. They won’t wait for you to get ready.

✦   ✦   ✦

Two lines, every day. Show up before you feel like it. The mood will occasionally arrive on its own. The method is what you do while you wait.
